## 0. Independence note

## 3. TDR-0040 — seat/spawn cardinality, seat resolution, per-seat row identity

### 3.1 Challenge points put to the record

1. **Is "one spawn ⇔ one seat" a decision or a restatement of the harness?** The record grounds it in two independent facts — `opencode run --help` exposes single-valued `--agent`/`--model` (spec Appendix C.1) and a `session_v2` row holds one agent/model — and in delivered practice (`scripts/deliver-ticket.sh:1740,1743`; `scripts/ceo-loop.sh:559,561`; `scripts/opencode-session.sh:337`). I re-read `HarnessSpawnInput` (`packages/core/src/contracts/harness.ts:268-272`, exactly `{workItemRef, prompt, descriptor}`) and the production composition (`packages/cli/src/production.ts:1773`, `spawnArgs: ["run", "--format", "json"]`). One invocation cannot truthfully claim N seats, so the cardinality rule is forced by the surface, not chosen against it. Accepted.
2. **Why not let one dispatch hold N sessions (ALT-4)?** Accepted: a dispatch attempt is one lease, one attempt record and one held identity (`packages/core/src/app/dispatch.ts:529`, nomination returns only `{profile}` at `packages/core/src/app/dispatch.ts:176-188`); making a run hold N sessions is a dispatch-contract change, which the record correctly refuses to do inside a harness-plane story. ALT-4's rejection on C-5 is sound.
3. **Does the resolution chain handle a present-but-empty agent identity?** **No — and this is the material finding.** Decision 2 states: *"Two outcomes are refused or classified, and only these"* (TDR-0040 line 290) — an `unassigned` refusal and the `no-agent` state. The chain's third step is *"the profile's single declared seat"* with no non-empty precondition. Spec v1.4 has since added a third outcome: *"the pure resolution function, which returns the binding, an `unassigned` refusal, or … an invalid-identity refusal (DEC-17)"* (`chg-GH-335-spec.md` line 129), with DEC-17 (line 409) and AC-F3-1 (line 443) requiring that a present empty/whitespace-only identity **refuses** with the delivered `config.value.invalid` and is **never** sent as `--agent ""`. The record carries **no** mention of `DEC-17`, `AC-F5-7`, `absentObservedAxes` or an unstated observed axis (verified by grep over the file). A coder implementing the record literally on a **single-seat** profile binds `""` to the sole seat and sends `--agent ""` — which the record's own C-1 and A-1 forbid.
4. **Does the record cover AC-F5-7's unstated observed agent?** Not fully. Decision 4 scopes the binding to *"the group whose observed agent equals the binding's agent"* and gives `unresolved-seat` to unbound runs and non-matching groups; AC-F5-7 (spec line 454) additionally requires that a group whose observed agent is **unstated** degrades to a null seat with `unresolved-seat`. The record predates that criterion and does not state it, while claiming to be the single home of seat identity.
5. **Does the record claim anything the delivery cannot meet?** I specifically tested the failure mode named in the commissioning brief (a claim that the shipped `dispatch` propagates an agent). It does not: A-4 says *"The shipped `dispatch` sets neither member (DEC-15): its spawns reach the port in the `no-agent` state, bind nothing, and are disclosed `unresolved-seat`"*, matching spec DEC-15 (line 407) and plan Phase 4.3 (lines 1463-1470). No impossible claim found here.
6. **Are the facts real?** Re-read and confirmed: `targetSeatName` fallback returns the alphabetically first seat (`packages/core/src/provider/profile.ts:124-131`); a seat's model is validated only as a non-empty string (`packages/core/src/provider/validate.ts:501-502`, `nonEmptyString(value.model)`); `provider.profile.invalid` exists with a seat-naming remediation (`packages/core/src/contracts/error-table.ts:218-226`); `HARNESS_FAILURE_CODES` holds exactly nine codes (`packages/core/src/contracts/harness.ts:294-304`); the step record carries `agentRole` and `model` (`packages/core/src/contracts/telemetry.ts:211-230`). The "High" confidence is justified **for the facts**.

## 5. TDR-0042 — opencode integration mode

### 5.1 Challenge points put to the record

1. **Are the three modes the observed surfaces, and is the selected one observed to run?** Yes: the help output in `chg-GH-335-spike-evidence.md` (lines 35-48) shows `--standalone`, `--server`, `service`, `serve`, `api`, `acp`; the `PATH` entry is a 98-byte wrapper that execs `opencode --standalone "$@"` (line 29). The record names the wrapper as the reason the selected mode is today's behaviour (context fact 5). Accepted.
2. **Does the record "describe rather than change" the delivered behaviour?** **Only on the observation host.** The harness help says `--standalone` means "Run with a private server **instead of the background service**" (spike evidence line 39), i.e. the harness's own default is the background service. On an adopter host without the wrapper, today's `dispatch` runs the background-service path; selecting `per-invocation-private-server` explicitly (C-2 forbids inferring it from the wrapper) **changes** the transport there and adds a private-server start per invocation. Driver 3's "described rather than changed" is therefore host-conditional and the record does not say so.
3. **Is the mode configuration expression honest?** Yes: `harness.mode` omitted → default with `defaults` origin; present and recognised → `configured`; unknown → delivered `config.value.invalid` naming the path; a recognised non-default mode refuses `harness.mode.unavailable` naming requested and available; no fallback (C-1…C-3). This matches spec AC-F2-1/AC-F2-3/AC-F2-4 and TDR-0041's mapping (`mode` = requested on a mode refusal).
4. **Is the vocabulary single-authored?** The record names three literals (`per-invocation-private-server`, `managed-background-service`, `running-server-client`); the test plan's summary and §5.1 use the shorter labels `standalone`, `background-service`, `server-client` (`chg-GH-335-test-plan.md` lines 10, 42) while asserting equality to `DEFAULT_HARNESS_MODE` (line 702). No literal conflict is asserted today, but two descriptive vocabularies exist; Phase 2 must pin the record's literals, not the test plan's labels, or TC-HARNESS-010's constant equality fails.
5. **Is the argv placement pinned?** Not literally. The criterion asserts `--standalone` "exactly once on its invocation"; because the observation host's wrapper prepends it, the **binary** may see it twice. Readiness iteration 2 records that `--standalone` is also available on `run`, so the risk is low, but the record does not state where the flag goes relative to `run`.
6. **Does the read-only store rationale hold?** Yes and it is grounded in the observed store (`chg-GH-335-spike-evidence.md` §4: read-only open with `PRAGMA query_only=1`, 30 tables, two generations) and in NG-5/NG-11. No write, no configuration mutation, no service lifecycle. Accepted.

## 6. TDR-0043 — adapter contract (window, mode, propagation by reference, observed-store telemetry)

### 6.1 Challenge points put to the record

1. **Is the window a rule with a delivery value, not a frozen pair?** Yes: C-1 states "the current minor line at delivery time plus one previous minor" and its value `2.3.x` + `2.2.x`, derived from the installed `opencode v2.3.2` (spec Appendix C; spike evidence line 14), with the constant carrying the rule and its revisit note. That is the only shape compatible with AC-F1-4's "no key that widens or narrows it" and DEC-1's time-relative rule. Accepted — with a maintenance risk (35-R1).
2. **Is the probe grammar falsifiable, and can a near-miss widen acceptance?** Yes: C-2 fixes product token + optional `v` + three-part semver, newline-terminated; everything else is indeterminate; a near-miss that parses is out of window (`2.20.0`, `12.2.5`). Accepted. One residual: the grammar is token-agnostic by design (brand neutrality, spec §21), so a binary reporting a different product token is accepted as in-window; the kind is known from configuration, so this is deliberate, not a hole.
3. **Can the delivery actually read the store it claims?** Verified both ways: the delivered extractor assumes absent columns — `work_item_ref` (`packages/adapters/src/harness/telemetry-opencode.ts:66,137`), `harness_version` (`:67`), `part.tool` (`:289`), and per-message `agent`/`model` in the tool query (`:289`); while the observed store (spike evidence §4) has two generations and none of those columns. The record's DM-10-shaped field set (C-8) matches the observed columns, and the delivered `skipped-gap`/`session-attribution-unavailable` outcome it cites exists (`packages/core/src/domain/closeout.ts:1021`). Accepted.
4. **Is the absence representation safe on the read side?** C-9 requires a group with an unstated observed axis to **land** with `absentObservedAxes`, the empty-string placeholder in the delivered non-null field, and `optional-field-absent`, superseding `invalid-session-identity` for that case only. Required by AC-F5-7. But `agent`/`model` are the delivered group key (`sameSessionGroup` compares `runId`, `sessionId`, `agent` and `model`, `packages/core/src/state/harness-telemetry.ts:44-52`) and spend's `role`/model dimensions, so an unstated axis becomes an **empty-keyed** group and an empty bucket on the read side. No read-side suppression is declared.
5. **Does the record honour its own C-5 ("the propagation rules are referenced, not restated")?** Partly. C-5 says a restatement "here is a defect of this record", yet C-6 restates the expressibility predicate and refusal that TDR-0040 decision 3 and implementation step 1 also state, and C-7/C-9 restate the binding-attachment/group-scoping rule that TDR-0040 decision 4 owns. Plan Phase 1.3 assigns "the expressibility-only refusal" and "the recorded binding" to this record and makes the *seat/carrier* rules reference-only (plan lines 844-873), so the duplication is **cross-record**, not clearly this record's fault — but exactly one home must remain.
6. **Is the option space real?** Three alternatives (delivered contract; configurable window + catalogue; selected). ALT-1 bundles two independent changes, so rejecting it does not independently test the window form or the catalogue; the nullable-field option, the delivered fail-the-group behaviour, and a per-generation reader are not evaluated as separate alternatives. The R2 minimum (ALT-0 plus ≥2 substantive alternatives) is met, but several independent choices in the bundle are not individually challenged.
7. **Does the record claim delivery it cannot achieve?** No. I tested the two likely over-claims: it does **not** claim an agent-naming `dispatch` (C-5 cites TDR-0040/DEC-15 and adds no obligation), and its "no catalogue, no second child process" rule (C-6) matches DEC-14 and AC-N1-1. Its file set maps onto plan Phases 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8.

## 8. Stakeholder harm

- **The learning/spend plane** consumes seat-attributed cost and tokens as facts. A wrong seat (32-R1) is a financial and planning misattribution, not a cosmetic label; this is the strongest harm in the set and the reason the DEC-17 gap should not be left to a coder's reading.
- **Operators declaring multi-seat profiles without `agentOverrides`** now get `provider.profile.invalid` instead of a silently borrowed seat. Deliberate and documented (spec OQ-5, TDR-0040 unresolved question); the harm is a configuration refusal, which is the intended outcome.
- **Operators on hosts without the `--standalone` wrapper** get a changed transport and an added private-server start per invocation (34-R1).
- **Operators who upgrade the harness ahead of a release** are blocked until the window is re-pinned (35-R1).
- **Read-side consumers** that treat `""` as a value rather than a marker see an extra empty dimension member (35-R2).
- **Privacy / safety / ethical:** no material harm found. Rows and disclosures carry identifiers, counts, cost and durations only; store reads are read-only with the query-only pragma before the first read; the deny-by-default spawn environment is preserved and the binding is an argument, not an environment variable (spec §20/§21, NFR-5/NFR-9, spike evidence §4). No content projection, no store write, no network.

## 9. Confidence assessment

- **TDR-0040 — High confidence is justified for the facts, not for the record as the complete seat home.** The harness surface, the store shape, the missing input members and the profile mapping are all repo-local or directly observed. The record's own residual assumptions (does the harness reject an unknown agent; are operator models `provider/model`-shaped) are labelled and bounded. What is **not** justified is the record's implicit claim to be the single authoritative home while it predates DEC-17 and AC-F5-7.
- **TDR-0041 — justified.** Every load-bearing fact was re-read in the tree (closed detail type, edge projection, the live dropped `eligibility`, validator/fixture divergence, exact-key pin, scripted key set, no attempt record on a pre-session refusal). The two remaining unknowns are labelled (external strict consumer; suiteBreadth ownership).
- **TDR-0042 — justified for the selection; over-confident for "describes rather than changes".** The surfaces are observed and the chosen mode is the one the observation host runs. The claim is host-conditional (34-R1) and should be read as "matches the observation host's installed wrapper", not as a general property.
- **TDR-0043 — justified for the contract; the "High" does not cover the maintenance burden**, which the spec correctly carries as RSK-2 rather than as record confidence. The reference boundary (C-5) is the one internal inconsistency.
